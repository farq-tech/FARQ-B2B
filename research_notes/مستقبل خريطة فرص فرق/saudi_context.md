# Saudi context for Farq Opportunity Map

## What reliable sources show about Saudi SME digital adoption and B2B procurement

### Takeaway
Saudi establishments are strongly connected and government procurement is digitally discoverable, but this does not establish that private-sector buyers use any single discovery or procurement workflow. Farq should treat a map as a discovery and research layer, not assume it replaces qualification, tendering, or relationship-led purchasing.

### Cited Findings
- GASTAT's 2023 establishment survey reported internet access for 97.7% of establishments, dedicated business email for 94.1%, social-media use for 57.7%, dedicated mobile phones for operations for 70.3%, and e-government-service use for 91.3%. The publication says the survey follows a methodology aligned with UNCTAD guidance. [GASTAT, *Establishments ICT Access and Usage Statistics 2023*](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/d/guest/ictbusiness-2023-en-1-pdf)
- GASTAT explicitly counts orders received through WhatsApp Business, Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat among online purchase orders in its establishment ICT survey. This establishes that the national measurement framework treats social messaging/platform orders as online orders; it does not quantify WhatsApp's share. [GASTAT, *ICT Access and Usage Statistics for Establishments*](https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/w/eyys)
- Monsha'at's current classification defines micro enterprises as 1--5 full-time employees and SAR 0--3m revenue; small as 6--49 and over SAR 3m to SAR 40m; medium as 50--249 and over SAR 40m to SAR 200m. If the two criteria differ, the higher category applies. [Monsha'at, *Enterprises Definition*, updated 21 October 2024](https://www.monshaat.gov.sa/en/node/4998)
- The World Bank's 2022--23 Saudi Enterprise Survey includes a question on an establishment's main supplier-chain/procurement technology. Its microdata catalogue shows raw case counts for that question (1,573 valid records) and warns they must not be interpreted as population summary statistics. This is useful evidence that procurement practice is heterogeneous and measurable, not evidence for national adoption rates. [World Bank Enterprise Surveys, Saudi Arabia 2022 variable SAd4](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/6499/variable/V102)
- For public-sector procurement, the Saudi Human Resources ministry describes Etimad as an electronic government financial-services platform launched in 2018 that standardizes and facilitates purchasing across government sectors; it supports viewing tenders, invitations, tender-document purchase, offer evaluation and award electronically. [HRSD, *Competitions and Purchases*](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/ministry/about-ministry/competitions-and-purchases)
- Etimad's public tender list supports filters including main activity, region (all 13 administrative regions) and price bands. This is an example of structured opportunity discovery in the public market, not a source for private-sector demand. [Etimad, *Tender list*](https://monafasat.etimad.sa/tenders/index)
- The Saudi Ministry of Commerce says the commercial-register law is intended to require periodic updating and make registered data available and easily searchable; the announcement concerns the legal register, not a guarantee that any third-party directory has complete, current operating-location data. [Ministry of Commerce, 17 September 2024 announcement](https://mc.gov.sa/en/mediacenter/News/Pages/17-09-24-01.aspx)

### Inferences
- **Buyer flow:** support Arabic searches for need/category, region, budget band, and supplier type, then make verification and shortlisting explicit. A buyer may research digitally yet still need phone, site, certification, capacity, commercial-registration, and quote checks before buying.
- **Seller flow:** a profile should surface what buyers can qualify (commercial name/registration where lawful and supplied, services, coverage area, evidence, contact route, last-confirmed date), while avoiding a claim that all listed firms are active or suitable.
- **Market-exploration flow:** the public-tender data model makes activity and region useful starting filters. Treat private-market opportunity estimates, buyer intent, and supplier availability as research outputs with coverage/confidence labels.
- **Marketing-targeting flow:** GASTAT's connected/mobile figures support a mobile-capable business product, but do not prove a particular user segment, job role, or outreach channel will convert. Segment by declared business purpose and geographic/service coverage before using contact data.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found that quantifies how Saudi private-sector SMEs discover new B2B suppliers, use map/search products, or choose among calls, WhatsApp, referrals, directories, marketplaces, and tenders. Do not state a dominant private B2B procurement channel without primary research.
- No reliable source found for Farq's target verticals that measures false positives, duplicate branches, stale contacts, or the conversion from map discovery to a qualified meeting. These must be measured on Farq's own candidate data.
- Interview and field-test questions: "Tell me about the last supplier you found outside your existing network"; "Where did you begin, what did you trust, and what did you have to verify?"; "What is the smallest set of proof needed before you contact a seller?"; "What fields make a location unusable?"; "Would you share demand, budget, or contact details in a discovery product, and under which permission?"

## What reliable sources show about WhatsApp, personal-data rules, Arabic/RTL/mobile use, and location intelligence

### Takeaway
WhatsApp can be a handoff channel only with an auditable consent model; it is not permission to message discovered phone numbers. Saudi privacy rules likewise require marketing consent and an opt-out path. Official national-address infrastructure offers a strong location reference, but Farq still has to validate entity-to-place matching and freshness.

### Cited Findings
- Meta's WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy says a business may contact a person only when the person supplied the mobile number and opted in to receive subsequent WhatsApp messages; the opt-in must clearly state WhatsApp messages and the business name. The policy requires honoring block, stop, and opt-out requests. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy/preview?lang=en_US)
- For the WhatsApp Business Platform, Meta says business-initiated conversations may begin only with an approved message template. A business may reply without a template within 24 hours of the user's last message. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy/preview?lang=es_LA)
- Saudi PDPL defines personal data broadly enough to include addresses, contact numbers, licences/records and property-related identifiers when they identify a person directly or indirectly. It generally requires consent to process personal data or change its purpose, subject to listed exceptions. [SDAIA, *Personal Data Protection Law*, arts. 1 and 5](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL)
- PDPL Article 25 bars use of a person's postal or electronic address and other personal contact means for marketing or awareness materials unless the recipient consents and the sender provides a clear way to stop; Article 26 permits marketing processing of non-sensitive personal data when collected directly from the person with consent. [SDAIA, *Personal Data Protection Law*, arts. 25--26](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL)
- The PDPL implementing regulations describe consent as freely given, clear about specific purposes, documented for later verification, and independent for each processing purpose. They require a controller to stop direct marketing without undue delay when consent is withdrawn. [SDAIA, *PDPL Implementing Regulations*](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/Research/Documents/ExecutiveRegulations.pdf)
- GASTAT reported that 99.0% of individuals used the internet in 2025 and 98.4% used a mobile phone to access it. This is population-level context, not evidence of a B2B user's preferred work device. [GASTAT, *ICT Access and Usage Statistics by Households and Individuals 2025*](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/20117/2435267/ICT%2BAccess%2Band%2BUsage%2B2025-EN.pdf/d91fc7dd-2f2e-ee1a-248c-c8f38102ef01?t=1767603674645)
- Saudi Digital Government Authority guidance explicitly notes that Arabic is read right-to-left and discusses mobile-screen constraints. It is government service guidance, but is a credible basis for including RTL and small-screen usability in Saudi product validation. [DGA, *Guideline: Digital Experience Maturity for Government Services*](https://dga.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2022-08/Guideline%20Digital%20Experience%20Maturity%20for%20the%20Government%20Services.pdf)
- Saudi Post SPL describes the National Address as geographical/location data for individuals, corporate and government sectors; its National Address API supports search by phone number, geocode, free text and short address. SPL says the underlying databases can support studies and commercial services. [SPL, *National Address API*](https://splonline.com.sa/en/national-address-api/)
- SPL says the National Address covers the Kingdom and advertises one-square-metre accuracy. That is a claim about the address system's geographic precision, not about merchant POI completeness, business category correctness, or contact accuracy in a third-party map. [SPL, *National Address*](https://splonline.com.sa/en/national-address-1/)

### Inferences
- **WhatsApp handoff:** show a click-to-WhatsApp action only after the prospective recipient has opted in to that business's WhatsApp communications, or design it so the user initiates the conversation with a seller. Store consent source, wording/version, scope/category, timestamp and withdrawal status; honor suppression across downstream campaigns.
- **Marketing targeting:** do not upload/scrape discovered personal mobile numbers for outbound WhatsApp campaigns merely because a business is on the map. Separate (a) business location discovery, (b) account/contact enrichment, and (c) an opted-in campaign audience; each needs an identified lawful basis and provenance review.
- **Arabic and mobile:** Arabic-first includes real RTL layout, Arabic search/query behavior, Arabic place/category aliases, Arabic numbers/dates where appropriate, and compact mobile workflows. English support can remain useful for company names, expatriate staff and registered legal names, but should be tested rather than presumed.
- **Location intelligence:** retain source, capture date, normalised national-address/geocode fields, entity/branch relationship, confidence, and verification status. Present uncertainty honestly: a pin is a candidate location until corroborated.

### Gaps
- The official sources establish policy and infrastructure; they do not establish how often Saudi B2B users want to be contacted by WhatsApp, whether they prefer Arabic-only flows, or which consent experience they find credible. These are interview/usability-test hypotheses.
- Legal implementation needs Saudi counsel for Farq's exact controller/processor role, data sources, cross-border transfers, retention, and whether a particular business contact is personal data in the actual context. This note is product research, not legal advice.
- Validate with interviews and prototype tests: "Would you rather initiate a WhatsApp conversation yourself, receive a WhatsApp follow-up, call, or request a callback?"; "What wording would make WhatsApp permission clear in Arabic?"; "How do you identify the right branch when legal name, trade name and map pin differ?"; "Which Arabic/English spelling or district terms do you search for?"; "How recent must a profile verification be before you trust it?"

## What Farq must treat as hypotheses, risks, and product-validation work

### Takeaway
The evidence supports building for connected, mobile-capable Saudi businesses and a compliant, confidence-aware map. It does not support claims that Farq has accurate data, solves procurement discovery, improves targeting performance, or that WhatsApp outreach will be welcomed or permitted for a discovered contact.

### Cited Findings
- GASTAT's establishment survey samples economic establishments across sectors and reports access/usage indicators by activity; it is evidence about nationwide establishment ICT conditions, not about Farq customers, buyers, sellers, conversion, willingness to pay, or product-market fit. [GASTAT methodology, 2025](https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/w/methodology-and-quality-report-for-ict-access-and-usage-statistics-for-establishments-2025)
- The World Bank catalogue's explicit warning against reading raw microdata case counts as population statistics means it cannot substantiate a national claim about the share of Saudi firms using manual search, spreadsheets, SRM, or e-procurement. [World Bank Enterprise Surveys, Saudi Arabia 2022 variable SAd4](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/6499/variable/V102)
- WhatsApp's policy conditions messaging on consent and allows enforcement for policy violations; Saudi PDPL conditions marketing contact and requires stopping when consent is withdrawn. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy/preview?lang=en_US); [SDAIA PDPL Implementing Regulations](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/Research/Documents/ExecutiveRegulations.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Core hypotheses to test, not assertions:** (1) buyers have a repeated pain finding locally capable suppliers beyond their existing network; (2) sellers value being discoverable to relevant buyers; (3) geographic and category signals predict a real commercial opportunity; (4) a verified profile reduces qualification time; (5) an Arabic-first, mobile workflow improves completion; (6) users will initiate or explicitly opt in to WhatsApp contact; (7) businesses will pay for discovery, intelligence, leads, or targeting.
- **Research design:** recruit buyers and sellers across Riyadh, Jeddah/Makkah region, Eastern Province and at least one non-metro region, plus micro/small/medium firms and priority verticals. Run 8--12 contextual interviews per role/vertical until decision patterns stabilize; then test a clickable Arabic RTL prototype on mobile with task completion, trust, error and consent-comprehension measures. These are proposed methods, not a claim that the sample is statistically representative.
- **Data-quality acceptance tests:** take a stratified candidate set and measure exact legal/trade-name match, duplicate rate, reachable and role-correct contact rate, branch-vs-HQ correctness, pin-to-entrance distance, category accuracy, operating status, and age of confirmation. Publish only fields that pass defined confidence thresholds and give users a correction/report route.
- **Trust/risk controls:** label source and last verified date; distinguish public tender signals from inferred private demand; protect non-public contact data; allow seller claim/correction; log consent; support Arabic export/print/share flows only where data permissions allow; and give users a clear "why this result" explanation for any score or ranking.

### Gaps
- Farq's initial customer segment, vertical, data sources, geographic coverage, commercial model, and outbound operating model were not provided. These are required before selecting success metrics or making a defensible market-size/revenue claim.
- No source reviewed establishes a right to scrape, reuse, sell, or combine any particular directory/map/social-platform dataset. Obtain contractual/API rights and a privacy review for every source before ingestion or redistribution.
- Decision gates before launch: buyer task success versus baseline; seller claim/accuracy rate; contact consent and opt-out rates; data-quality acceptance thresholds; proportion of opportunity signals that users confirm as relevant; and evidence of willingness to pay. Until these are measured, describe all benefit and performance statements as hypotheses.
